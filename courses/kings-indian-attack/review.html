King’s Indian Attack — Combat Review

25 recognition scenarios. Do not reveal the answer until you can state: Black’s plan, your central break, your worst piece, and the condition that would cancel your plan.

Scenario 1: Lesson 001 — The KIA Is a System, Not a Move Sequence

Situation: You are tempted to play Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2, e4 regardless of Black.

Reveal field answer

Order: Aim for the familiar shell, but let Black's center decide the order and whether you should complete it at all.

Why: The value of the KIA is recurring structures and plans, not automatic moves.

Failure condition: If Black can seize the center freely or force tactics, the diagram is no longer the mission.

Scenario 2: Lesson 005 — The e5 Wedge Is a Strategic Weapon

Situation: Black has a stable pawn on d5/e6 structure and you can advance e4-e5 without losing the pawn.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play e5 when it gains space, drives a defender, and fixes Black's kingside structure for an attack.

Why: A closed center gives you time to maneuver knights and pawns toward the king.

Failure condition: Do not play e5 if Black can immediately demolish it with ...f6 or ...d4 under favorable tactics.

Scenario 3: Lesson 011 — The 1.e4 Route Is Best Against ...e6 Structures

Situation: Black answers 1.e4 with ...e6 or reaches an e6-Sicilian.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use d3, Nf3/Nd2, g3, Bg2 and castle; prepare e5 when Black plays ...d5.

Why: Black's light bishop is often restricted, making your kingside space gain more meaningful.

Failure condition: If Black has already developed that bishop outside the chain, do not assume the same attack will be as strong.

Scenario 4: Lesson 013 — Against 1...e5, Do Not Pretend It Is a French KIA

Situation: Black meets 1.e4 with ...e5 or establishes ...e5 in one move from a flexible Nf3 route.

Reveal field answer

Order: Switch to a normal open-game/Réti plan; fight the center directly instead of playing a slow e4-d3 shell by reflex.

Why: Black has not lost time or locked in the c8 bishop, so the classic KIA attacking conditions are weaker.

Failure condition: Autopilot d3-g3 can concede the center without gaining the structural targets the KIA relies on.

Scenario 5: Lesson 014 — Against an Early ...c5, Ask Whether ...e6 Follows

Situation: Black plays a Sicilian ...c5.

Reveal field answer

Order: If Black commits to ...e6, the KIA becomes highly natural; if Black chooses ...d6 or ...Nc6 without ...e6, be ready for a more central c3-d4 plan.

Why: The exact Sicilian structure changes which knight square and pawn break matter most.

Failure condition: Do not use one anti-Sicilian recipe against all Sicilians.

Scenario 6: Lesson 020 — Choose c3-d4 When Black Holds Back ...d5

Situation: In an e6-Sicilian, Black has a small center and delays ...d5.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use c3 followed by d4 when you can seize the center without allowing a favorable exchange.

Why: The KIA is not only an e5 attack; sometimes your best punishment is simply occupying d4.

Failure condition: If you lock yourself into Nbd2 too early, you can make the d4 break clumsy.

Scenario 7: Lesson 021 — Against ...e6 and ...d5, e5 Is the Main Territorial Gain

Situation: Black has the French-style center and you are ready to support e4.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prepare and play e5 when tactically sound; then organize pieces behind the kingside space.

Why: The e5 wedge cramps f6/d6 squares and makes Black's kingside defense harder.

Failure condition: If e5 can be destroyed immediately by ...f6 or ...d4 with gain of time, improve support first.

Scenario 8: Lesson 023 — Meet ...f6 as a Central Counterstrike, Not a Surprise

Situation: Black attacks your e5 wedge with ...f6.

Reveal field answer

Order: Calculate exf6, keeping tension, or reinforcing e5 based on piece activity and king safety.

Why: Black must challenge e5 or remain cramped; ...f6 is a thematic liberation attempt.

Failure condition: Do not automatically capture if opening the f-file helps Black more than you.

Scenario 9: Lesson 025 — h4 Is a Hook-Creating Move, Not Decoration

Situation: The center is closed and Black's king is castled short.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use h4-h5 when it can provoke ...h6, ...h5, or open the h-file/g6 square for pieces.

Why: A pawn hook gives your pieces something concrete to attack.

Failure condition: Do not push h-pawn if Black can open the center with tempo and your pieces are not ready.

Scenario 10: Lesson 027 — Black's Queenside Expansion Is a Clock

Situation: Black has played ...b5-b4 or is preparing ...a5-a4 while your king attack is forming.

Reveal field answer

Order: Count tempi: attack only if your kingside threats arrive before Black opens files on the queenside.

Why: KIA positions are often races; positional beauty does not matter if the opponent's break lands first.

Failure condition: Do not ignore a hanging c2/b2 pawn or a file opening just because your attack looks thematic.

Scenario 11: Lesson 031 — Against an e6-Sicilian, First Identify Black's d-Pawn

Situation: Black has ...c5 and ...e6, but has not committed the d-pawn.

Reveal field answer

Order: If ...d5 comes, prepare the e5 wedge; if ...d6 stays, consider c3-d4.

Why: The d-pawn tells you whether the position is French-like or small-center Sicilian.

Failure condition: Do not commit your knight/pawns before you know which central break you need.

Scenario 12: Lesson 033 — If Black Plays ...d6, Prepare c3-d4

Situation: Black keeps a compact c5-d6-e6 center.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use c3 and d4 when you can build a full center; delay Nbd2 if it obstructs that plan.

Why: Black has given you time because ...e6 and ...d6 used two moves to restrain the center.

Failure condition: Do not spend that extra time on slow wing moves while d4 is available.

Scenario 13: Lesson 035 — If Black Stops d4 With ...e5, Switch to Queenside Space

Situation: You prepared c3-d4 and Black answers with ...e5.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use the closed center to expand with b4/a4 or maneuver for f4, depending on piece placement.

Why: Black spent ...e6-e5 and fixed the center, giving you time and clear pawn-chain targets.

Failure condition: Do not keep forcing d4 if it only creates a weak pawn or opens the g7 bishop.

Scenario 14: Lesson 041 — The Caro-Kann Is Not a French With ...c6

Situation: Black plays ...c6 and ...d5.

Reveal field answer

Order: Locate the c8 bishop before choosing the classic e5 attack.

Why: If the bishop escapes to f5/g4, Black has solved one of the French's main problems.

Failure condition: Do not assume identical plans from similar-looking pawn chains.

Scenario 15: Lesson 043 — If ...Bf5 Is Outside the Chain, Attack the Center

Situation: Black's light bishop is active on f5.

Reveal field answer

Order: Build e4 and challenge d5; consider Nh4 only if winning the bishop or gaining time serves the center.

Why: The bishop's activity reduces the payoff of a slow kingside attack.

Failure condition: Chasing the bishop with pawns can create holes without gaining anything.

Scenario 16: Lesson 049 — When the Center Opens, Stop Calling It a KIA

Situation: Central exchanges remove the e4/d3 shell and open files.

Reveal field answer

Order: Reassess by normal chess principles: activity, open files, weak pawns, king safety.

Why: Systems are training maps; open positions demand concrete piece play.

Failure condition: Do not waste tempi recreating the shell after the structure has transformed.

Scenario 17: Lesson 051 — Against a Three-Pawn Center, Attack the Base

Situation: Black owns c5-d5-e5.

Reveal field answer

Order: Strike with c4, d4, or e4 pressure at the base rather than launching h-pawns.

Why: Broad centers are strong only while they can move together.

Failure condition: A flank attack that ignores the center gives Black the easiest possible plan.

Scenario 18: Lesson 054 — If Black Closes With ...d4, Use the New Outposts

Situation: Black advances d5-d4 and fixes the center.

Reveal field answer

Order: Attack the pawn chain with c3/c4 or maneuver to c4/e4; use the closed center to choose a wing.

Why: A locked center gives time but also defines permanent weak squares.

Failure condition: Do not mechanically play e5 if it simply seals in your own bishop and gives Black more space.

Scenario 19: Lesson 061 — Attack Only After You Have a Fixed Target

Situation: Black has castled short and the center is closed.

Reveal field answer

Order: Identify a pawn hook or weak square—h6, g6, f7, h7—then organize pieces around it.

Why: Attacks succeed when your force has a specific point of entry.

Failure condition: Do not push pawns randomly without knowing which file or square you intend to open.

Scenario 20: Lesson 065 — f4 Is Usually a Lever, Not an Automatic Pawn

Situation: Your pieces can support f4 and the center is closed enough.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play f4 when it challenges e5/f5 squares or prepares f5 without opening your king prematurely.

Why: The f-pawn can add a second front to the attack.

Failure condition: Moving it weakens e3/g3 and the king; do not push before checking ...d4, ...c4, or tactical shots.

Scenario 21: Lesson 071 — The Dark Bishop Usually Stays on g2

Situation: You are considering exchanging Bg2 for a knight or bishop without structural gain.

Reveal field answer

Order: Keep the bishop unless the exchange wins a key square, pawn, or defender.

Why: It protects your king and often becomes your strongest piece after central exchanges.

Failure condition: Trading it can create permanent dark-square weaknesses around your king.

Scenario 22: Lesson 081 — Black's ...b5-b4 Is a Real Attack

Situation: Black gains queenside space and threatens to dislodge c3/d2 pieces.

Reveal field answer

Order: Decide whether to ignore and race, slow it with a4, or strike the center before the file opens.

Why: Your attack is only justified if it is faster than Black's counterplay.

Failure condition: Do not call queenside moves 'irrelevant' because your king is on g1.

Scenario 23: Lesson 083 — Meet ...d4 by Finding the Break Against the Chain

Situation: Black fixes a pawn on d4.

Reveal field answer

Order: Attack its base with c3/c4 or pressure e5/c5; maneuver to squares the pawn no longer controls.

Why: Closed chains create static targets and outposts.

Failure condition: Do not keep trying e5 if the real strategic battle is now d4/c5.

Scenario 24: Lesson 091 — Opening Success Means a Playable Middlegame

Situation: You have reached move 10 with no tactical problems and a clear plan.

Reveal field answer

Order: Stop judging the opening by engine decimals; judge whether you understand the next break, worst piece, and opponent threat.

Why: A system is valuable when it gives you repeatable decisions.

Failure condition: Do not waste tempi trying to 'prove' an advantage that the structure does not contain.

Scenario 25: Lesson 100 — Final Doctrine: System Rules End Where Concrete Chess Begins

Situation: A familiar KIA pattern conflicts with a tactical fact or a stronger positional break.

Reveal field answer

Order: Choose the move demanded by the board, even if it abandons the KIA shape completely.

Why: The purpose of opening study is to reach positions you understand—not to preserve an opening identity.

Failure condition: Never sacrifice good chess to remain 'in the KIA.'
