1. The White Hippo Is a Setup With an Extra Tempo

Situation: You want the same low, flexible structure from the White side.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Build a double fianchetto with d3/e3, knights usually on d2/e2, and keep the option to break with c4, e4, f4, b4, or g4.

Reason: White's extra tempo means you should eventually use the shell to claim something, not simply mirror Black forever.

Abort / warning: Do not waste the first-move advantage on purposeless waiting.

2. Nd2 Keeps c- and e-Pawn Options Open

Situation: Your queenside knight needs a square while you delay c4/e4.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use Nd2 to support e4/c4 and keep c3 free for a pawn if needed.

Reason: The square fits the shell and gives later routes through f1, c4, or b3.

Abort / warning: Do not leave the knight buried on d2 after the center opens.

3. Black Builds a Big e5-d5 Center

Situation: Black occupies both central squares while you remain low.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prepare c4 or e4 to challenge the center before it gains more space.

Reason: A Hippo shell is useful because it stores pawn breaks; it is not permission to let Black own the whole board.

Abort / warning: Do not spend three more tempi on rook-pawn moves while the center becomes fixed against you.

4. Black Plays ...h5 Early

Situation: Black wants ...h4 or a kingside hook.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Stop ...h4 if it is annoying, or strike in the center while Black spends time on the wing.

Reason: A wing pawn move is slow unless it creates a concrete threat.

Abort / warning: Do not weaken g3/h3 automatically unless you know which file will open.

5. Use e4 to Claim the Center

Situation: Black has not fixed e4 and your pieces support the advance.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play e4 when it gains space, opens the g2-b7 diagonal after future exchanges, and does not walk into a strong ...d5 hit.

Reason: White can transform the shell into a King's-Indian-Attack-like center with one timely pawn move.

Abort / warning: Do not push e4 just because it is available; know how you will answer ...d5.

6. Use g4 Only With a Closed Center

Situation: Black's king is short-castled and the center cannot be opened against you quickly.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prepare g4 when it gains space, supports h4-h5, or chases a key defender.

Reason: Kingside pawn storms are powerful when your own king is protected by a closed center.

7. Against a Classical ...e5 Center

Situation: Black has ...e5 with normal development.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use d3 and c4 or a timely e4/d4 challenge, depending on which break attacks the base without blocking your bishops.

Reason: A center pawn is strong only while its support chain is stable.

Abort / warning: Do not attack the head of the chain if the base can be undermined.

Situation: Black fianchettoes and waits for your center.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use your extra tempo to take a useful center with e4, d4, or c4 rather than mirroring passively unless you have a specific reason.

Reason: When both sides are flexible, White should usually claim something with the extra move.

Abort / warning: Do not turn the game into a pure mirror and hand the initiative back.

9. Use the Extra Tempo in a Mirror

Situation: Both sides have nearly identical low shells.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Choose one purposeful break—usually c4, e4, or f4—before Black has time to do the same.

Reason: The first break can force the opponent to react and destroy the symmetry on favorable terms.

Abort / warning: Do not spend the extra tempo on a third waiting move.

Reveal field answer

Order: Decide whether d4, c4, or e4 gives you the best pawn chain and bishop activity; do not merely defend.

Reason: The opponent's break is an invitation to choose the transformation you prefer.

13. Castle Before Starting a Pawn Storm

Situation: You want f4, g4, or h4 and the center might open.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Secure the king first unless the position gives a concrete reason not to.

Reason: The same pawn that attacks can also remove your king's shelter.

Abort / warning: Do not launch a storm with the king still on e1 in a fluid center.

14. Opposite-Side Castling Changes Everything

Situation: You and Black castle on opposite wings.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prioritize pawn speed and open files toward the enemy king; king-side pawn shelter becomes expendable if it is your attacking wing.

Reason: Opposite castling makes tempi more valuable and defensive pawn moves more dangerous.

Abort / warning: Do not use slow knight reroutes if a pawn race has already begun.

16. Open Center Means Reassign Every Piece

Situation: Pawn exchanges remove the small-center structure.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Move knights to active central squares, put rooks on open files, and stop preserving old shell squares.

Reason: Once the structure changes, old piece-placement rules expire.

Abort / warning: Do not keep a knight on e2 or d2 if an open file demands immediate centralization.

17. When Black Pins With ...Bg4

Situation: Black develops ...Bg4 and pins a knight or provokes h3.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Ask whether the pin actually prevents your intended break; break the pin with h3 only when the bishop matters, otherwise develop and keep your pawn structure intact.

Reason: Pins are often invitations to waste a tempo chasing a bishop.

Abort / warning: Do not weaken h3/g3 automatically if the pin is harmless.

18. When ...c5-c4 Tries to Clamp You

Situation: Black pushes c5-c4 and gains queenside space.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Attack the base with b3, dxc4 when legal, or central expansion e4/d4 depending on the structure.

Reason: A space-gaining pawn on c4 can also become a hook and leave d4/b4 squares behind it.

Abort / warning: Do not spend several moves moving pieces away while the clamp becomes permanent.

19. The Ten-Second White Hippo Scan

Situation: It is your move in the shell.

Reveal field answer

Order: Ask: What did Black threaten? Can I claim center now? Which break opens a bishop? Is my king safe? Which piece is worst?

Reason: The scan turns a passive-looking system into active decision training.

20. If Black Stops e4, Use c4 or d4

Situation: Black has arranged strong control of e4.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Do not force e4; use another central lever that attacks the same structure from the side.

Reason: The shell preserves multiple pawn breaks so one blocked route does not end your plan.

Abort / warning: Do not spend several tempi preparing a break Black has already over-defended.
