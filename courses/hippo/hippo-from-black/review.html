1. The Hippo Is a Shape, Not a Sequence

Situation: You are tempted to play the same ten setup moves regardless of White.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Aim for the shell, but change the order whenever White creates a concrete threat or forces the center to open.

Reason: A system survives because its pieces and pawns cooperate, not because the moves were memorized in one order.

Abort / warning: Do not sacrifice development or king safety just to complete a diagram.

2. Do Not Rush ...a6

Situation: You have seen ...a6 in every Hippo diagram and want to play it automatically.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play ...a6 when it stops a useful Bb5+, prepares ...b5, or serves as a safe waiting move after urgent development is handled.

Reason: Rook-pawn moves are useful because they preserve the core center, but they do not develop a piece.

Abort / warning: If you are behind in development, a decorative ...a6 can be one tempo too slow.

3. The Triple-Center Warning

Situation: White has pawns on c4, d4, and e4 with room to keep expanding.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Stop trying to complete the Hippo on autopilot; consider an early ...c5, ...d6 with ...Nf6, or another Modern/KID/Benoni-style transformation.

Reason: A huge three-pawn center can gain too much space if Black spends several more tempi on quiet shell moves.

Abort / warning: Do not confuse universality with invulnerability; sometimes the correct Hippo skill is leaving the Hippo.

4. Bc4 Targets the Hippo's Soft Spots

Situation: White develops Bc4 and points at e6/f7.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Complete ...e6 and development with tactical care; if the bishop becomes exposed, gain time with ...d5 only when the center supports it.

Reason: The bishop on c4 is active but can also become a target when the center closes.

Abort / warning: Do not play a pawn break that opens the c4-g8 diagonal against your king without calculation.

5. The e5 Rule: Answer With ...d5

Situation: White advances e4-e5 in a normal closed Hippo position.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Usually meet it with ...d5 if tactically sound, fixing the center and preparing ...c5 against d4.

Reason: The lock limits White's center and gives the cramped side clear breaks.

Abort / warning: Do not open the position for White's better-developed pieces unless you gain something concrete.

6. Do Not Lock Your Own Bishop Out

Situation: A thematic lock is available, but it would bury one of your bishops behind fixed pawns.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Before locking, identify which bishop will become bad and whether it has a useful square or trade.

Reason: A closed center is only helpful if your pieces still have routes to improve.

Abort / warning: Do not create a permanent bad bishop without a plan to exchange or reroute it.

7. Use ...c5 Against a d4 Anchor

Situation: White's center is built around d4 and the c-file can be opened.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prepare ...c5 to attack d4 and challenge White's queenside space.

Reason: The break attacks the base of the pawn chain and can activate the b7-bishop and c-file.

Abort / warning: Do not play ...c5 if White can answer d5 and completely shut your pieces out without compensation.

8. When ...b5 Is Correct

Situation: White has castled queenside or committed pieces to the queenside and your center is stable.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Prepare ...b5 with ...a6 and sometimes ...Rb8, using the b-file as the attack lane.

Reason: The Hippo can attack either wing; the opponent's king tells you where the space gain matters most.

Abort / warning: Do not push ...b5 if cxb5 simply wins a pawn and leaves no recapture or pressure.

9. Ne7-g6 Is a Standard Route

Situation: The center is closed and the e7-knight has no immediate central jump.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Route it to g6 where it supports ...f4, ...f5, and pressure on e5/h4.

Reason: The Hippo knight becomes dangerous when it stops being a passive defender and joins the chosen wing.

Abort / warning: Do not send it to g6 if White can win it with h5 and there is no retreat.

10. Rb8 Belongs With ...b5

Situation: You have chosen queenside counterplay.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use ...Rb8 when the b-file may open or when it materially strengthens ...b5.

Reason: Rooks should stand behind the pawn break they are meant to exploit.

Abort / warning: Do not play ...Rb8 as a waiting move if no b-file will ever open.

11. Against the Austrian-Style f4 Center

Situation: White builds e4, d4, and f4 with attacking intent.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Accelerate central control and king safety; prepare ...e6, ...Ne7, ...d6 and a timely ...f5 or ...c5 rather than completing slow extras.

Reason: White's center is powerful but also gives you clear pawn targets and break squares.

Abort / warning: Do not spend tempi on both rook pawns while White is ready for e5.

12. Against an Early Bc4

Situation: White's bishop eyes e6 and f7.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Build e6 carefully, consider gaining time on the bishop after the center closes, and watch tactical sacrifices on e6.

Reason: An active bishop can be a target if its supporting center becomes fixed.

Abort / warning: Do not weaken f7 or open the diagonal toward your king unnecessarily.

13. Against 1.d4 and 2.c4

Situation: White builds a queen-pawn center without e4.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: You can still use a Hippo shell, but keep ...c5 and ...f5 transformation options in mind and do not insist on a slow full setup if White gets a free broad center.

Reason: The structure is universal only if you respect the different pawn targets created by c4.

Abort / warning: Do not apply e4-based break rules to a position where the e-pawn never moved.

14. Against a White Fianchetto

Situation: White also develops g3 and Bg2.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Do not mirror without purpose; identify which center break gives your bishops a better diagonal first.

Reason: Symmetry favors the side who breaks at the right moment, not the side who copies moves longer.

Abort / warning: Do not assume a mirrored bishop setup means the position will stay quiet.

15. Castle Before the Center Opens

Situation: White has a prepared central break and your king remains on e8.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: If castling removes tactical checks and does not walk into a wing attack, castle before the break lands.

Reason: The fastest way to lose a Hippo is to discover that the closed center was only temporarily closed.

Abort / warning: Do not spend the last safe tempo on a rook-pawn move.

16. b6 Can Become a Hook

Situation: Your b6-pawn supports the bishop but White has a4-a5 or c4-c5 ideas.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Watch whether White can fix or attack b6 and open the a- or b-file.

Reason: The queenside fianchetto creates long-term dark-square strength but also a pawn target.

Abort / warning: Do not protect b6 with more pawns if active counterplay solves the problem faster.

17. French-Like Transition After e5/...d5

Situation: White e5 and your ...d5 lock create a French-style chain.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Think like a French defender: undermine d4 with ...c5, pressure the chain base, and improve the bad bishop.

Reason: Recognizing the new structure gives you a ready-made middlegame plan.

Abort / warning: Do not keep using pre-lock Hippo waiting moves after the position has become a French structure.

18. Queen Trades Often Reduce the Space Problem

Situation: White offers a queen trade while you have less space but a sound structure.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Consider the trade favorably if it removes attacking chances and leaves your pieces routes to improve.

Reason: With fewer pieces, cramped positions become easier to manage.

Abort / warning: Do not trade queens if the resulting endgame leaves a permanently bad minor piece and weak pawns with no counterplay.

19. The Ten-Second Hippo Scan

Situation: It is your move in a familiar-looking shell.

Issue the order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Ask: What did White threaten? Which central pawn moved? Is my king safe? Which break is available? Which piece is worst?

Reason: A fixed scan prevents autopilot and forces you to react to the position instead of the opening name.

Abort / warning: Do not move until you can answer the first question: what changed on White's last move?
