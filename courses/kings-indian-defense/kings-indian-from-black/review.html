1. The KID Is Counterattack, Not Passivity

Situation: White owns more central space after e4 and d4.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Finish development, castle, then challenge the center with ...e5 or ...c5 instead of defending the space disadvantage forever.

Reason: The KID accepts space so Black can attack the pawn center and the king later.

Abort / warning: If you never strike the center, White keeps the space with no price.

2. Do Not Mix ...e5 and ...c5 Without a Reason

Situation: You are tempted to push both central pawns because both are thematic.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Decide which structure you want first; add the second break only when it improves that specific structure.

Reason: Every pawn move changes squares, diagonals and targets permanently.

Abort / warning: Two thematic moves played in the wrong order can leave holes without creating counterplay.

3. The Classical Break: ...e5

Situation: White has Nf3 and Be2 and has not prevented ...e5.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play ...e5 and make White declare the center.

Reason: The move attacks d4 indirectly, claims dark squares and prepares a closed KID after d5.

Abort / warning: Check tactics on e5 and d4 before treating ...e5 as automatic.

4. Know When ...a5 Helps

Situation: White’s b4 expansion is the main queenside lever.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Play ...a5 when it fixes b4 or forces White to spend time preparing the pawn advance.

Reason: A single restraining move can slow the queenside without abandoning your kingside plan.

Abort / warning: Do not push ...a5 if it simply gives White b5 with tempo and a protected passer.

5. 9.Ne1 Signals the Classic Race

Situation: White retreats the knight and prepares f3/Nd3.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Treat the position as a race: organize ...f5 quickly while watching c5 and b4.

Reason: Ne1 supports the center and clears f3 for a pawn; White accepts time to solidify.

Abort / warning: Do not answer a slow maneuver with even slower waiting moves.

6. The h-Pawn Usually Joins Last

Reveal field answer

Reason: A second pawn lever is strongest after pieces are ready to exploit opened files.

Abort / warning: Do not spend two tempi on the h-pawn while White’s c5 break is landing.

Reason: The Bayonet saves White tempi compared with slower maneuvers and attacks your pawn-chain base.

8. Do Not Defend b7 With Your Whole Army

Situation: White has opened queenside lines against b7/a7.

Reveal field answer

Order: Give material only if the kingside attack is concrete; otherwise contest files with pieces rather than passive pawn defense.

Reason: KID counterplay dies when every piece turns backward.

Abort / warning: A pawn can be expendable; your initiative cannot be.

9. Petrosian: White Closes Before Castling

Reveal field answer

Order: Do not rush the normal ...Nc6-e7 route; develop with ...Nbd7, ...a5, or ...Na6 according to the position.

15. Why the Fianchetto Is Different

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Expect less direct kingside access; plan central and queenside counterplay with patience.

Reason: White’s king is well defended and the g2 bishop pressures the queenside/center.

17. Against the London, Strike the Center

Situation: White plays Bf4/e3 without c4/e4.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Use ...d6 with ...e5 or ...c5 to challenge d4; do not build a closed KID shell against a center that is not there.

Reason: London structures give Black different pawn targets from the Classical KID.

Abort / warning: Opening name does not justify ...e5-f5-g5 autopilot.

23. Field Test: ...e5 or ...c5?

Situation: White has e4/d4 and you must choose the first central strike.

Issue your order before opening the answer.

Reveal field answer

Order: Choose ...e5 for a closed kingside-race structure when legal; choose ...c5 when d4 is the immediate target or the variation discourages ...e5.

Reason: Break selection determines the middlegame you are volunteering to play.
